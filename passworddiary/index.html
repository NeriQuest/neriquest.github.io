AES-256 Envelope Encryption

Every secret field (passwords, CVVs, account numbers, private keys) is encrypted with an isolated 256-bit vault key using AES-256 in CBC mode (marked with ENC:v1: cipher prefix). Data is never stored in plain text anywhere on disk.

256-Bit Vault Key Unique Random IVs Local Ciphertext

🛡️

PIN Key-Wrapping & Zero Data Loss

Your Master PIN (4 to 6 digits) combines with cryptographic random salts to wrap (encrypt) the master vault key. When you change your Master PIN, your vault key is instantly re-wrapped without needing to re-encrypt every single database record!

Max 6-Digit PIN Instant Key Re-wrapping Hardware Biometrics

Encrypted Google Drive Sync

Cloud backup is 100% encrypted before leaving your phone. Backups are stored strictly in your own Google Drive. Google and third parties see only scrambled cipher payloads. Includes an option to permanently purge remote backups anytime.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know

Clear, direct answers about PasswordDiary encryption, Google Drive sync, and Master PIN security.

PasswordDiary uses military-grade AES-256 Envelope Encryption. Your passwords, card credentials, and notes are never saved in plain text. Instead, they are transformed into unreadable ciphertext (using random IVs and CBC mode) before being written to your local phone database or uploaded to Google Drive.

No, never. Even when you backup to Google Drive, the backup file is fully encrypted with your vault key into an unreadable scrambled payload before it leaves your device. Without the key, the file is impossible to read. Neither Google, nor third parties, nor the app developers can decrypt or view your secrets.

PasswordDiary uses a 4 to 6-digit Master PIN. This PIN combines with cryptographic random salts to wrap (encrypt) your master 256-bit vault key. If you change your Master PIN later in Settings, your vault key is instantly re-wrapped with zero risk of database corruption or data loss!

Whenever you launch PasswordDiary with Google Drive enabled, the app checks if the remote backup file differs from your local vault. If items were edited on another device or added locally, it displays a clear summary (e.g., "2 new on Google Drive • 1 new on Device") and offers a 1-tap bidirectional merge so no entry is ever lost.

Yes. If you decide to go fully offline or want to wipe your cloud copy, PasswordDiary provides a direct option in Settings to permanently purge all backup data from your Google Drive with immediate verification.

The app continuously audits the entropy of every saved credential. If any password lacks sufficient length, complexity, or character diversity, it flags it with a warning badge. You can open the Weak Passwords sheet anytime to review and strengthen them using the built-in generator.
